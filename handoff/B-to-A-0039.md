## What

In un-paused play the clay and night figures are coarse and stepped and the number on the back cannot be read, while the ice, the boards and the HUD in the same
frame are sharp; every frozen still shows crisp figures. Lane SK20 (`status/SK20-report.md`, stills `devlog/shots/20261113-2017*-B-sk20-*`) found the cause by
switching one thing at a time in fresh un-frozen sessions of the same seeded match:

- **The game renders at 1398 x 790 and is shown at 1920 x 1085** (a GPU profile in the play session names the pass `TemporalSuperResolution(...) 1398x790 -> 1920x1085`).
  `r.ScreenPercentage` is 0 and `r.ScreenPercentage.Default.Desktop.Mode` is 1, so the engine picks the fraction from the display size; the project sets neither.
- TSR is meant to rebuild the detail from earlier frames, but **it rejects its history on the moving figures** (`ShowFlag.VisualizeTSR 1`: the figures are red in the
  History Rejection panel), so they show the 790-line picture enlarged. A frozen figure stops moving, TSR keeps its history, and five seconds later it is crisp:
  that is why every still of today looked better than play.
- Nothing else changed the steps: mip bias and streaming, motion blur, TSR history at 200, TSR flickering rejection, velocity vertex deformation, the felt off,
  the rim off, TAA. The figure materials use no dither, depth offset, position offset or mask.

**The ask**, in `Game/Config/DefaultEngine.ini`, section `[/Script/Engine.RendererSettings]`:

```
r.ScreenPercentage.Default.Desktop.Mode=0
```

Proof: with this set as a console variable (plus `r.Editor.Viewport.OverridePIEScreenPercentage 0` so the editor session takes the game's path) the profile reads
`1920x1085 -> 1920x1085` and the steps are gone, clay and night. Cost on B's machine in the editor, one run each: clay 54.0 to 51.0 frames a second, night 54.7 to
53.8. Not tested: the line as an ini line, a package, full screen at a higher resolution (there you may prefer a fixed `r.ScreenPercentage` by quality level
rather than 100 % everywhere: the figures are what suffers below about 85 %).

On B's side: the clay figure's back number is dark ink and larger now (it was a pale digit with a thin outline on cream): it reads at the game camera's 7 m in
play at either resolution.

## Why

It is the difference between the game the operator has been shown in stills all day and the game he plays.
