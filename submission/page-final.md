# HOKEJ

## A. The one-line pitch

Hockey with the whistle taken out: clay skaters, and the speed you bring is the hit you get.

## B. The 60-word blurb

HOKEJ is rough five-a-side hockey for clay skaters, with the whistle taken out. The mouse is your stick. The speed you bring is the hit you get: a nudge beside a man, a flattening head-on. Stop five stick lengths short of a defender, cut, go. Twelve modes change the rules. Once a period, you move one dial. Nobody is polite.

## C. The full page

*Hockey with the whistle taken out.*

You skate, and the mouse is your stick. Five clay skaters a side on a clay rink, timber stands along the boards, a clay crowd in them, and nobody blows a whistle. One law is left: the speed you bring is the hit you get. Drift beside a man at his speed and your check is a nudge. Meet him head-on at full speed and he is on the ice. When it is you on the ice, you get up.

### First minute

Press PLAY NOW. Your first match is at ROOKIE, the newcomer's level; the switch under the button also reads PRO and ALL-STAR, and the level you light is the level you get. Blue against orange, and you are the blue one under the yellow chevron. Skate with W A S D. The blade follows your cursor on the ice. Hold the left button to load a shot, let go to fire. Skate at a carrier and press the right button. Get hit back. Get up. Q passes. A panel at the bottom left lists the keys all match. By the first goal you will know whether this is your game.

### Three things this game says

1. **The speed you bring is the hit you get.** One button, three sizes: a bump, a stumble, or down for a second or two, by the speed between the two bodies. Beside a man at his speed: a nudge. Head-on at full speed: the biggest hit in the game. A skater who stands still holds a hit from the side. Carry the puck and you cannot check, so the carrier runs. Nothing is drawn on you while you are down, and for two seconds after you get up a hit on you is a bump at most.
2. **The puck is yours until someone presses for it.** That is the rule: it leaves you by a shot, a pass, a poke, a shove or a check. Win it and a yellow ring closes under you: for that half second no stick can take it. Want his? Wait for his ring to close, then Space jabs for the puck. About one jab in ten comes away with it, and a miss beside a carrier locks your stick for a moment.
3. **Stop. Then go.** Press S against your own motion (or hold Ctrl) and you brake hard; the first step after a stop is quick. Charge straight at a defender who is set for you and you do not get through. Stop five stick lengths short of him, cut, go: that works, and from the same distance at every level.

### What the ice tells you

A ring in the other team's colour round a loose puck: one of them arrives when you do. Take it and you may be hit; brake short, or let him have it and hit him. A loose puck inside the ring under you is yours, wherever your stick points. Hold a pass and the mate who will get it has his ring turn your colour. Nothing else is drawn on the ice: no line ahead of you, no mark at a hit.

### Three levels

- **ROOKIE.** "New to hockey games? Start here: rookies are a step slower." A newcomer wins most of these. If you have played a hockey game before, you will win it, quickly.
- **PRO.** "Played one before? Pros hit as a habit." About even for someone who has.
- **ALL-STAR.** "The wall. All-stars hunt the puck. Beat pro first."

The quoted lines are the menu's own. The level you light plays PLAY NOW and every mode; the season's takes bring their own, and the pause card tells you which one you are in.

### The season

Eight takes: The Read-Through, The Big Room, Rough Cut, Set Pieces, Second Wind, The Last Word, Slow Motion, The Final Cut. The first four are a school on the clay rink against rookies. Then the lights change (neon, night), the bots turn pro, and the finale is against all-stars. Said plainly: a newcomer feels the step at take five and hits a wall at take eight; someone who has played before walks through seven and meets the wall at the eighth.

### The bots

Five a side, and they play as a side: your mates pass to you and go for loose pucks; the other five carry while the lane is open, pass when you close in, brake on purpose and sprint with the puck. They also lean on a net's frame now and then (see the limits).

### Twelve modes, and the Director

A mode is a rule set written as data, no code. Pick one under MODES.

- **Arsenal**: grab a boost, get a trick on F: a shotgun, a hip check, a shield or a slow puck. Each trick fires three times.
- **Bruisers**: five a side, heavy bodies. Hit.
- **Cage**: a tight box, eight bodies, four pillars on the ice. The boards hit back.
- **Fog of War**: three a side, night and fog, bots that react late. Slip by, then hit.
- **Glow Ice**: neon ice, the only whistle left. Foul, sit ten seconds.
- **Marathon**: three on three on endless ice. Bring your legs.
- **Outnumbered**: three on five. Keep the puck, take the heat. F is a shield.
- **Pond**: pond hockey, five a side. Pass, skate, nobody gets hit.
- **Power Play**: five on three, and the five are yours. Move it, fire it, repeat.
- **Shotgun**: tight ice, ten bodies. F is one blast that clears the lane: anyone near its path goes down. Yours too.
- **Sniper**: five on four, a big net, a low guard. Lift it, bury it.
- **Sudden Death**: five a side, one goal ends it. The meanest bots alive.

The Director also lives inside the match. Esc pauses it. Once a period CHANGE A RULE is yours: move one slider (how fast everyone skates, how hard the shot flies, how slippery the ice is, how sharp the bots are), press APPLY, and the scoreboard tells everyone the new rule.

MAKE YOUR OWN, under MODES, takes one sentence from you. Without a key it answers from the games it made earlier, or you pick one of the sentences on the screen. With your own key (typed under SETTINGS, never bundled with the game) the Designer writes a new game from your sentence.

### Two machines

PLAY WITH A FRIEND: one of you hosts, the other types the invite code, both on the same network. Each of you gets a full seat: your own sprint, pass, brake, spin, check and F, and the bots on your side plan with you as a human. The two games stay identical frame by frame. We have played it as two copies of the game on one machine, through the real lobby, to the final card and into a rematch, and a thousand soak matches never drifted. Two physical machines have never been tried.

### The rink

A clay rink with timber stands along both sides and a clay crowd in them. Benches, penalty boxes, clay goal frames. Score, and a red lamp lights over the net and the net shakes. Throw someone into the boards and the boards rock. Later takes and some modes change the scene: neon, night, fog.

### Controls

| Action | Keyboard and mouse | Pad |
|---|---|---|
| Skate | W A S D or arrows | left stick |
| Aim the stick | the mouse (the blade follows the cursor on the ice) | right stick |
| Shoot, with the puck (a tap is flat; hold to lift it, let go to fire) | left mouse button | RT |
| Check, without the puck (come in fast or it is a bump) | right mouse button | RB |
| Cross-check, without the puck: shove the man in front off it; hold to brace | left mouse, middle mouse or mouse 4 | RT or LB |
| Pass, with the puck: to the mate your stick points at; hold for power | right mouse button or Q | RB or X |
| Ability (the mode's trick or a pickup's, when the slot is lit) | F | R3 |
| Spin off a man who is on you | E | B |
| Deke | mouse wheel | Y |
| Sprint | Shift | LT |
| Brake hard | S against your motion, or Ctrl | stick back, or L3 |
| Poke: jab for the puck (about one in ten) | Space | A |
| Team signals | 1 2 3 4 | D-pad |
| Restart | R | View |
| Pause: resume, change a rule, controls | Esc | Start |

The same list is in the game under CONTROLS and on the pause card.

### Sound

A booth with a line for most things that happen to you, shown as captions on the screen.

### How it was made

Made with Unreal Engine 5.9 (C++ gameplay, Blueprint visuals, Niagara, MetaSounds). The code, the tests, the build gate and the dev log were written and run by AI coding agents directed by the team; the rules on this page were measured in thousands of simulated matches, and the limits below are those measurements. The dev log (Glass Box) is the whole story, turn by turn. ### Known limits, said plainly

- Hammering the right button lands more checks than timing it. We know.
- At pro you spend real time on the ice, most often in the moment you win the puck. The ring round a loose puck is the warning.
- The stop beats one defender. It does not win you a match by itself.
- Under pressure your passes still arrive only one time in three to one in two.
- On the big rinks you can skate half a minute without touching the puck.
- A skater can stand at a downed body and push it along the ice.
- A bot can lean on a net's frame for a second or two before it goes round.
- After a steal, a carried puck can sit inside another skater for a few frames.
- The season is a school and then a wall, with no step between for someone who has played before.
- Bruisers and Cage play closer to the regular game than their cards promise.
- At the side boards the camera goes steep and close, and you can lose the rink.
- The pad is mapped. No person has play-tested it.
- Cancel on the firewall prompt: we expect it to be fine for solo play, and it is not tested. The prompt is there because PLAY WITH A FRIEND opens a socket.
- The joined match is played on one machine, never yet across two.

### How to run

Unzip (about 283 MiB) (the Director names the zip at 22:30, R-0073), run `ThirdJam.exe` (the game's internal project name; the game is HOKEJ). Windows 10 or 11 (DirectX 12) only. No web build. First launch compiles shaders for a few seconds. Windows may ask about the firewall the first time: solo play does not need network access, so we expect Cancel to be fine (not tested), and PLAY WITH A FRIEND needs Allow. If it will not start, run `Engine\Extras\Redist\en-us\vc_redist.x64.exe` once. `README.txt` in the zip says the same, with the controls.
